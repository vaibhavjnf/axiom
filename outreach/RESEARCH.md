## 3) Kickstarter hardware case studies (lessons, not copy)

### A. Pebble (smartwatch) — crowdfunding success vs later outcomes
- **What happened:** Massive Kickstarter validation for wearable hardware (often cited ~$10M+ campaigns in industry writeups).
- **Lessons for Axiom:** Crowdfunding proves demand for a *category* (wearable/desk companion) but does not replace DFM, battery/cert planning, or post-campaign cash for tooling + yield. Success metric = units delivered happily, not $ raised.
- **Sources:** Industry summaries e.g. https://www.fundreef.com/crowdfunding-for-hardware-startups-special-challenges/ ; TechCrunch pitfalls piece https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/10/3-pitfalls-that-can-sink-any-crowdfunded-hardware-startup/

### B. Oculus Rift (VR DK1) — Kickstarter → VC → acquisition
- **What happened:** ~$2.4M Kickstarter (2012) for developer kits; used as demand proof before large VC; later Facebook acquisition.
- **Lessons for Axiom:** Price and ship a *developer/early* kit with clear expectations; use campaign to recruit ecosystem partners (devs, reviewers); treat KS as marketing + validation, then raise for manufacturing scale.
- **Sources:** https://www.fundreef.com/oculus-rift-from-kickstarter-to-facebook-acquisition/

### C. Cross-cutting manufacturing lesson (Coolest Cooler vs winners)
- **What happened:** Some of the largest hardware campaigns failed to deliver; winners had production-ready designs and realistic COGS.
- **Lessons for Axiom:** Freeze enclosure RF + thermal before campaign; get factory quotes (Flex/mid-tier CM) and UN38.3 path *before* public pricing; budget 30–50% contingency vs prototype COGS (common industry guidance).
- **Sources:** https://www.jaycon.com/from-kickstarter-to-series-a-hardware-startup-funding-crowdfunding-and-manufacturing/ ; https://titoma.com/blog/kickstarter-to-mass-production-what-hardware-startups-miss/
